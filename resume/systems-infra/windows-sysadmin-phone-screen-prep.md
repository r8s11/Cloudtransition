## The VMware Question (high-priority — they WILL ask)

This is the gap. Don't lie, don't apologize, don't undersell. Here's the angle:

> "I want to be straight with you on VMware. I've worked with it across multiple roles — at Jounce I created and configured VMs to spec, and at Ventus and Peak I've supported VMware-hosted server infrastructure as part of broader systems work. I wouldn't position myself as a VMware specialist with 5 years of deep ESXi/vCenter administration. My deepest production strength is Windows Server, Active Directory, and M365. That said, I learn fast on infrastructure tooling, and if VMware depth is the bar, I'd want to understand what specific aspects of the environment matter most so I can speak to what I've actually done versus what I'd be ramping on."

**Why this works:**
- Honest (recruiter can't trip you up later)
- Names what you HAVE done
- Pivots to your real strengths
- Invites them to clarify what depth they actually need (often "5+ years" = "comfortable in vCenter, can do basic admin")

**Backup if pressed:** "I'd be happy to share specifics on what I've done with VMware — VM creation, console access, snapshot/template work, troubleshooting. If the role is more architecture-level (cluster design, DRS, vSAN), I'd be transparent that's an area I'd grow into."

## "Why Are You Looking?" / "What Have You Been Doing Since Peak?"

This is the gap-narrative question. Keep it confident and matter-of-fact.

> "After Peak Scientific wrapped up earlier this year, I made a deliberate decision to relocate to New York, start my B.S. in Computer Science at SNHU — I'm a couple of months in — and run a focused job search rather than jumping straight into the next thing. I had savings runway to do it intentionally. Now I'm fully focused on landing the right next role, and a Windows Sysadmin role like this one is exactly the direction I want to go: systems and infrastructure ownership, mixed Windows/Linux/cloud, real automation work."

**Why this works:**
- Frames the gap as **intentional**, not anxious
- Three concrete things you've been doing: relocating, school, structured search
- Pivots immediately to forward-looking "what I want next"
- Doesn't apologize, doesn't over-explain

**Don't:**
- Sound defensive about the gap
- Volunteer too much detail unprompted
- Mention any role that isn't on your resume

**If they push for more detail:**
> "Honestly, the move plus enrolling in school took real focus, and I wanted to be deliberate about the next step rather than reactive. I'd rather take the right role than the next available one."
