# Consulting Operations Runbook

**Companion to:** [Consulting Business Plan](2026-03-19-consulting-business-plan.md)
**Date:** 2026-03-19

This document defines repeatable processes for running your consulting practice. Follow these playbooks so you spend your time doing the work, not figuring out how to run the business.

---

## 1. Lead-to-Client Pipeline

### Stage 1: Inquiry Received
**Trigger:** Someone reaches out (Upwork message, LinkedIn DM, email, referral)
**Response time:** Within 4 hours during business hours, next morning otherwise

**Actions:**
1. Acknowledge the inquiry — "Thanks for reaching out. Let me ask a few questions to see if I can help."
2. Ask qualifying questions:
   - What's the problem or need?
   - How many users/employees?
   - What tools are you currently using?
   - What's the timeline?
   - Is there a budget in mind?
3. Log the lead in your tracking sheet (Google Sheet or Notion)

### Stage 2: Scoping
**Trigger:** Lead has a real need you can serve

**Actions:**
1. Schedule a 20-minute discovery call (free) — use Calendly or Cal.com (free tier)
2. During the call, listen more than you talk. Identify:
   - The root problem (not just the symptom they described)
   - Urgency level
   - Decision-maker (is this person the buyer?)
3. After the call, send a follow-up email within 24 hours summarizing what you heard

### Stage 3: Proposal
**Trigger:** Scoping call went well, client wants to move forward

**Actions:**
1. Match their need to a package (Section 3 of business plan) or create a custom scope
2. Write a simple proposal (1–2 pages max):
   - Problem summary (in their words)
   - What you'll deliver
   - What's NOT included (scope boundaries)
   - Timeline
   - Price
   - Payment terms (50% upfront for projects, prepaid for retainers)
3. Send as PDF, follow up in 3 days if no response
4. One follow-up max — if silence after two contacts, move on

### Stage 4: Engagement Start
**Trigger:** Client signs proposal / pays deposit

**Actions:**
1. Send contract (see Contract Checklist below)
2. Confirm deposit received before starting work
3. Set up communication channel — agree on email, Slack, or Teams
4. Schedule kickoff: 30-minute call to align on first steps
5. Create a shared folder (Google Drive or OneDrive) for deliverables

## 2. Project Execution Playbook

### For Fixed-Price Packages

| Phase | Actions | Timeline |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Kickoff** | Gather access/credentials, confirm scope, set expectations | Day 1 |
| **Execution** | Do the work, document as you go | Days 2–5 (typical) |
| **Checkpoint** | Mid-project update to client (email or quick call) | Midpoint |
| **Delivery** | Present deliverables, walk client through findings/setup | Day 5–7 |
| **Handoff** | Written documentation, any credentials/access created, close-out email | Day 7 |

### For Retainer Clients

**Weekly rhythm:**
- Monday: Check in with client — any open issues or upcoming needs?
- As-needed: Respond to tickets/requests within 4 business hours
- Friday: Log hours, update internal notes
- Monthly: Send health report (what was done, hours used, recommendations)

**Hour tracking rules:**
- Track every minute — Toggl running whenever you're working on a client
- If approaching hour limit mid-month, notify client proactively
- Unused hours do NOT roll over (keeps it sustainable)

## 3. Delivery Standards

Every engagement should meet these quality bars:

### Documentation Deliverables
- **Always leave a written artifact** — even if the client didn't ask for one
- Configuration changes: screenshot before/after + written summary
- New setups: step-by-step runbook the client can follow
- Audits: findings report with severity ratings (Critical / Important / Nice-to-Have)

### Communication Standards
- **Response time:** 4 business hours for retainer clients, 24 hours for project clients
- **Status updates:** At least 1 per week for active projects
- **Bad news early:** If something is taking longer or you hit a blocker, tell the client immediately — never surprise them at the deadline
- **Underpromise, overdeliver:** Quote 5–7 days, deliver in 3–4

### Handoff Checklist
Before closing any engagement:
- [ ] All deliverables sent to client
- [ ] Documentation provided (runbook, config notes, or audit report)
- [ ] Access/credentials documented and shared securely (not in email body)
- [ ] Client walkthrough completed (call or recorded video)
- [ ] Final invoice sent
- [ ] Testimonial requested (if engagement went well)
- [ ] Follow-up reminder set for 30 days (check in, plant seed for next engagement)

## 5. Contract Checklist

Every engagement needs a signed agreement covering:

- [ ] **Parties** — your name/business and the client's company
- [ ] **Scope of work** — specific deliverables and what's NOT included
- [ ] **Timeline** — start date, milestones, end date
- [ ] **Payment terms** — amount, deposit requirement, due dates, late payment policy
- [ ] **Intellectual property** — client owns all deliverables upon full payment
- [ ] **Confidentiality** — you won't share their data or configs
- [ ] **Liability limit** — capped at the contract value
- [ ] **Termination** — either party can cancel with 7 days written notice
- [ ] **Independent contractor status** — you are not an employee

**Template source:** Bonsai (free tier includes 1 contract template) or search "freelance IT consultant contract template" and customize.

## 6. Financial Operations

### Invoicing Rhythm
- **Fixed-price projects:** 50% deposit before start, 50% on delivery
- **Retainers:** Invoice on the 1st, due by the 5th, work begins after payment
- **Hourly overflow:** Invoice weekly or biweekly with Toggl time report attached

### Expense Tracking
Track monthly in a simple spreadsheet:

| Category | Examples | Deductible? |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| Software/tools | Toggl, Google Workspace, Bonsai | Yes |
| Home office | % of rent, internet, electricity | Yes (simplified method: $5/sq ft, max 300 sq ft) |
| Professional development | Certs, courses, books, conference tickets | Yes |
| Hardware | Laptop, monitor, peripherals | Yes (depreciated or Section 179) |
| Marketing | Domain, hosting, LinkedIn Premium | Yes |
| Travel | Client site visits, transit | Yes |

### Tax Obligations
- **Quarterly estimated taxes** — file Form 1040-ES (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15)
- **Self-employment tax** — 15.3% on top of income tax (this surprises new freelancers)
- **Rule of thumb:** Set aside 30% of every consulting payment in a separate savings account for taxes
- **NY state/city tax:** File as usual, consulting income goes on Schedule C

## 8. Scaling Triggers

Signs you're ready to level up:

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Consistently billing 10+ hrs/week for 2+ months | Raise hourly rates by $10–15 |
| Turning away work | Start subcontracting to another freelancer |
| 3+ retainer clients | Consider LLC formation |
| Tier 2 skills are solid (Docker, CI/CD, Terraform) | Update all profiles, raise rates to $85–$125/hr |
| Earning $3,000+/month consulting | Evaluate whether full-time job is still necessary |
