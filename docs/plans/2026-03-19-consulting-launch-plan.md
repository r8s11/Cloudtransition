# Consulting Launch Implementation Plan

> **For Claude:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task.

**Goal:** Go from zero consulting presence to first paying client within 6 weeks (April 1 – May 15, 2026).

**Architecture:** Three parallel workstreams — (1) Business Setup, (2) Online Presence, (3) Client Acquisition — executed in weekly sprints. Each week has a clear deliverable that builds on the previous week.

- [Operations Runbook](2026-03-19-consulting-operations-runbook.md)

## Week 1 (Apr 1–6): Foundation

**Theme:** Set up the business backbone. No selling yet — just get the infrastructure in place.

### Task 1: Get an EIN

**Steps:**
1. Go to https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online
2. Apply as sole proprietor, select "started a new business"
3. Save the EIN confirmation letter as PDF to `docs/consulting/ein-confirmation.pdf`

**Done when:** You have a 9-digit EIN number saved

**Time:** 15 minutes

---

### Task 2: Open a Separate Bank Account

**Steps:**
1. Open a free business checking account (Chase, Capital One, or your current bank)
2. Use your EIN (from Task 1) and your legal name as the business name
3. Set up a linked savings account — this is your 30% tax reserve account
4. Note account details securely

**Done when:** Checking + savings accounts are open, linked, and you can transfer between them

**Time:** 30–60 minutes (online or branch visit)

---

### Task 3: Set Up Invoicing (Wave)

**Steps:**
1. Create account at https://www.waveapps.com
2. Add your business info (name, EIN, address)
3. Create an invoice template with:
   - Your name and contact info
   - Payment terms: "Due within 15 days of receipt"
   - Bank transfer or Zelle info (no checks)
4. Create a test invoice to yourself to verify formatting

**Done when:** You can generate a professional-looking invoice in under 2 minutes

**Time:** 20 minutes

---

### Task 4: Set Up Time Tracking (Toggl)

**Steps:**
1. Create free account at https://toggl.com
2. Create these projects:
   - "Business Development" (for non-billable time: proposals, outreach)
   - "Template – Client Work" (clone for each new client)
3. Install the browser extension and/or mobile app
4. Track 1 hour of setup time today to build the habit

**Done when:** Toggl is installed, projects created, and you've logged your first time entry

**Time:** 15 minutes

---

### Task 5: Create a Contract Template

**Steps:**
1. Sign up for Bonsai free tier (https://www.hellobonsai.com) OR download a freelance IT consultant contract template
2. Customize with your details and these key clauses:
   - Scope of work (fill-in-the-blank section)
   - Payment terms: 50% deposit for projects, prepaid for retainers
   - What's NOT included (explicit exclusions)
   - Liability capped at contract value
   - Independent contractor status
   - Either party can cancel with 7 days written notice
   - Confidentiality clause
3. Save as `docs/consulting/contract-template.pdf`

**Done when:** You have a reusable contract you can customize for any client in under 10 minutes

**Time:** 45 minutes

---

### Task 6: Set Up Scheduling (Calendly)

**Steps:**
1. Create free account at https://calendly.com
2. Create one event type: "20-min Discovery Call"
   - Duration: 20 minutes
   - Availability: Set 3–4 slots per week during evenings or lunch breaks
   - Buffer: 15 minutes before and after
   - Confirmation email: "Looking forward to learning about your IT needs!"
3. Copy your booking link — you'll use this in outreach

**Done when:** Someone can book a call with you via a link

**Time:** 15 minutes

---

### Week 1 Checkpoint

- [ ] EIN obtained
- [ ] Bank accounts opened (checking + tax savings)
- [ ] Wave invoicing set up with template
- [ ] Toggl tracking active
- [ ] Contract template ready
- [ ] Calendly scheduling link live

**Total time: ~3 hours**

## Week 2 (Apr 7–13): Online Presence

**Theme:** Make yourself findable and credible online. This is your storefront.

### Task 7: LinkedIn Profile Overhaul

**Steps:**
1. **Headline:** "IT Infrastructure Consultant | AWS & Azure Certified | Helping Small Teams Get Enterprise-Grade IT"
2. **About section** (write fresh, ~150 words):
   - Who you help (small/mid-size businesses without a full IT team)
   - What you do (M365 admin, security, infrastructure, cloud setup)
   - Why you (7+ years, AWS + Azure certified, bilingual)
   - CTA: "Book a free 20-min call: [Calendly link]"
3. **Experience:** Add "IT Infrastructure Consultant | Independent" as current role
   - List your 5 Tier 1 services as bullet points
4. **Skills:** Add/reorder: Microsoft 365, Azure, AWS, Active Directory, IT Security, Jira, ServiceNow, Python, Docker
5. **Featured section:** Add a link to your GitHub portfolio
6. **Settings:** Turn on "Open to Work" → set to "Providing Services"
7. **Banner image:** Use Canva (free) to make a simple banner with your name + "IT Consultant" + certifications

**Done when:** Profile looks like a consultant's, not a job seeker's. Ask a friend to review.

**Time:** 2 hours

---

### Task 8: Upwork Profile Setup

**Steps:**
1. Create account at https://www.upwork.com
2. **Title:** "IT Support Specialist | M365 Admin | AWS & Azure Certified"
3. **Overview** (~200 words):
   - Lead with the problem you solve: "Need enterprise-grade IT without the enterprise price tag?"
   - 7+ years experience, industries served (biotech, healthcare, professional services)
   - Certifications: AWS CCP, AZ-900
   - Bilingual (English/Spanish)
   - Services: M365 admin, security reviews, help desk setup, infrastructure troubleshooting
4. **Skills tags:** Microsoft 365, Azure, AWS, IT Support, System Administration, Active Directory, Network Administration, Jira, IT Security
5. **Hourly rate:** Set at $65/hr (slightly below market to land first reviews)
6. **Portfolio:** Add screenshots or descriptions of 2–3 relevant projects from your work history (anonymized)
7. **Availability:** Set to "More than 30 hrs/week" (even if you're doing less — it affects search ranking)

**Done when:** Profile is 100% complete and submitted for review

**Time:** 1.5 hours

---

### Task 9: Fiverr Pro Application

**Steps:**
1. Create account at https://www.fiverr.com
2. Create 2 gig listings:
   - **"M365 Health Check & Security Audit"** — $500, 5-day delivery
     - Description: what's included (from your packages in the business plan)
     - 3 deliverables: tenant audit report, security recommendations, licensing optimization
   - **"IT Help Desk Setup (Jira/Zendesk)"** — $1,200, 10-day delivery
     - Description: full help desk setup from scratch
     - 3 deliverables: configured system, SLA policies, training session
3. Apply for Fiverr Pro (requires work samples — use anonymized examples from past roles)

**Done when:** Both gigs are published and searchable

**Time:** 1.5 hours

---

### Task 10: GitHub Portfolio Page (Optional but Valuable)

**Steps:**
1. If you don't have one already, create a GitHub profile README
2. Add a "Consulting Services" section linking to your Calendly
3. Pin your best repos (WODCard, SalsaTD, dashboard-app)
4. Make sure each pinned repo has a clear README

**Done when:** GitHub profile shows technical credibility at a glance

**Time:** 30 minutes

---

### Week 2 Checkpoint

- [ ] LinkedIn profile fully updated for consulting
- [ ] Upwork profile live and submitted for review
- [ ] Fiverr gigs published (2 listings)
- [ ] GitHub portfolio cleaned up

**Total time: ~5.5 hours**

## Week 3 (Apr 14–20): Start Bidding & Posting

**Theme:** Active outreach begins. You're now selling.

### Task 11: First Upwork Bids (5 Proposals)

**Steps:**
1. Search Upwork for these keywords:
   - "Microsoft 365 setup"
   - "IT support small business"
   - "Office 365 admin"
   - "IT security audit"
   - "help desk setup"
2. Filter by: posted in last 24 hours, budget $200+, client has verified payment
3. Write 5 personalized proposals. For each:
   - Open with their specific problem (quote their job post)
   - 1–2 sentences on your relevant experience
   - Propose a concrete next step: "I'd suggest a 20-min call to scope this properly"
   - Keep under 150 words
4. Track bids in a simple spreadsheet: date, job title, bid amount, status

**Done when:** 5 proposals sent, tracked in spreadsheet

**Time:** 2 hours

**Repeat:** Send 3–5 proposals every Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday

---

### Task 12: First LinkedIn Post

**Steps:**
1. Write a post about your career transition. Example angle:
   > "After 7 years managing IT for biotech companies, I'm launching an independent consulting practice. Here's what I've learned about what small businesses actually need from IT support..."
   > [3–4 practical tips from your experience]
   > "If you're a small team struggling with IT, my DMs are open."
2. Keep it under 200 words, use line breaks for readability
3. Post on Tuesday or Wednesday morning (highest engagement)
4. Respond to every comment within 4 hours

**Done when:** Post is live, you've engaged with any comments

**Time:** 30 minutes

**Repeat:** Post every Tuesday or Wednesday

---

### Task 13: Research NYC MSPs for Subcontracting

**Steps:**
1. Search Google Maps for "managed service provider NYC" or "IT support company NYC"
2. Make a list of 10 small MSPs (under 50 employees — they're more likely to subcontract)
3. For each, note:
   - Company name and website
   - Services they offer
   - Contact email or LinkedIn of owner/manager
4. Save list to `docs/consulting/msp-outreach-list.md`
5. Do NOT reach out yet — this is Week 4's task

**Done when:** List of 10 MSPs saved with contact info

**Time:** 1 hour

---

### Task 14: Research NYC Networking Groups

**Steps:**
1. Search for BNI chapters in your area: https://www.bni.com/find-a-chapter
2. Search Meetup.com for: "small business networking NYC", "entrepreneurs NYC"
3. Look up NYC SCORE: https://www.score.org/newyorkcity
4. Identify 2–3 groups that meet weekly or biweekly
5. Register to attend 1 meeting in Week 4 or 5

**Done when:** Registered for at least 1 networking event

**Time:** 30 minutes

---

### Week 3 Checkpoint

- [ ] 5 Upwork proposals sent
- [ ] First LinkedIn post published
- [ ] MSP outreach list created (10 companies)
- [ ] Networking event registered

**Total time: ~4 hours**

## Week 4 (Apr 21–27): Expand Outreach

**Theme:** Double down on what's working, add MSP outreach.

### Task 15: Upwork Bidding Round 2 (5+ Proposals)

**Steps:**
1. Review Week 3 proposals — any responses? Adjust approach if needed:
   - If no responses: shorten proposals, lead with a stronger hook, lower rate slightly
   - If responses but no closes: improve your discovery call pitch
2. Send 5+ new proposals using same keywords
3. Try new keywords: "cloud migration", "Azure setup", "IT consultant"

**Done when:** 10+ total proposals sent across 2 weeks

**Time:** 1.5 hours

---

### Task 16: MSP Outreach (5 Emails)

**Steps:**
1. From your Week 3 list, pick 5 MSPs
2. Send a brief email to each:

   > Subject: Available for subcontracting — AWS/Azure certified IT consultant
   >
   > Hi [Name],
   >
   > I'm an independent IT consultant in NYC with 7+ years in infrastructure support (M365, AD, networking, security) and AWS + Azure certifications. I'm available for subcontracting if your team ever needs overflow support.
   >
   > I've worked across biotech and healthcare (HIPAA environments) and I'm bilingual (English/Spanish).
   >
   > Would you be open to a quick chat about how I could support your team?
   >
   > Best,
   > Roosevelt

3. Track responses in your outreach spreadsheet

**Done when:** 5 emails sent, tracked

**Time:** 1 hour

---

### Task 17: Second LinkedIn Post

**Steps:**
1. Pick a topic from your IT experience. Ideas:
   - "5 M365 security settings every small business should enable today"
   - "What I learned building a help desk from scratch at a 30-person biotech"
   - "The difference between good and bad IT support (from someone who's done both)"
2. Include 1 actionable takeaway the reader can use immediately
3. End with a soft CTA: Calendly link or "DM me"

**Done when:** Post is live

**Time:** 30 minutes

---

### Task 18: Attend First Networking Event

**Steps:**
1. Attend the event you registered for in Week 3
2. Bring nothing fancy — just your phone with LinkedIn and Calendly ready
3. Talk to 3–5 people. For each:
   - Ask about THEIR business first
   - When they ask what you do, use your blurb: "I help small businesses run their IT like a big company — without hiring a full-time IT person."
   - If there's interest, say: "Let me send you my LinkedIn — I've got a free 20-minute call if you ever want to chat about your setup"
4. Same evening: connect on LinkedIn with everyone you met, send a personalized note

**Done when:** Attended event, connected with 3+ people on LinkedIn

**Time:** 2–3 hours (including travel)

---

### Week 4 Checkpoint

- [ ] 5+ new Upwork proposals (10+ total)
- [ ] 5 MSP outreach emails sent
- [ ] Second LinkedIn post published
- [ ] Attended 1 networking event, made 3+ connections

## Week 5 (Apr 28 – May 4): Follow Up & Close

**Theme:** By now you should have some conversations going. Focus on converting.

### Task 19: Follow Up on All Open Leads

**Steps:**
1. Review your tracking spreadsheet
2. For every Upwork conversation that went quiet: send a polite follow-up
   > "Hi [Name], just circling back — still happy to help with [their project]. Let me know if you'd like to chat."
3. For every MSP that didn't reply: send ONE follow-up (then move on)
4. For LinkedIn connections from networking: send a value-add message
   > "Hey [Name], great meeting you at [event]. Saw this article about [their industry] and thought of you: [link]. Happy to chat IT anytime."

**Done when:** All open leads have been followed up

**Time:** 1 hour

---

### Task 20: Upwork Bidding Round 3 (5+ Proposals)

**Steps:**
1. Continue same cadence: 5+ proposals on relevant gigs
2. By now you should be refining your pitch based on what gets responses
3. If you've had any Upwork calls, write down what questions they asked — use these to improve future proposals

**Done when:** 15+ total proposals sent

**Time:** 1.5 hours

---

### Task 21: Third LinkedIn Post

**Steps:**
1. This week, try a "story" format post:
   - "Last year at [previous company], we had a major [incident]. Here's how I fixed it and what every small business can learn from it..."
2. These perform well because they're concrete and personal

**Done when:** Post is live

**Time:** 30 minutes

---

### Task 22: Run a Discovery Call (If Available)

**Steps:**
1. If you have a scheduled discovery call, follow the Scoping playbook from the Operations Runbook:
   - Listen more than you talk
   - Identify root problem, urgency, and decision-maker
   - Take notes
2. Within 24 hours, send follow-up email summarizing what you heard
3. Within 48 hours, send a proposal (use the template from the Runbook)

**Done when:** Call completed, follow-up sent, proposal delivered

**Time:** 1–2 hours

---

### Week 5 Checkpoint

- [ ] All open leads followed up
- [ ] 5+ new proposals (15+ total)
- [ ] Third LinkedIn post published
- [ ] Discovery call completed (if opportunity arose)

**Total time: ~4–5 hours**

## Week 6 (May 5–11): Land the First Client

**Theme:** Close a deal. If nothing has converted yet, expand tactics.

### Task 23: Evaluate & Adjust

**Steps:**
1. Review your metrics:
   - Upwork: proposals sent, response rate, calls booked
   - LinkedIn: post impressions, DMs received, connections made
   - MSP outreach: response rate
   - Networking: contacts made, follow-ups sent
2. Identify what's working and what isn't
3. If no traction on Upwork: try different keywords, lower rate to $55/hr for first gig (build reviews)
4. If no traction on LinkedIn: try different post formats (polls, questions, shorter tips)
5. If MSPs responded positively: schedule calls this week

**Done when:** Honest assessment written, adjustments made

**Time:** 30 minutes

---

### Task 24: "First Gig" Discount Push

**Steps:**
1. If you haven't closed yet, offer a limited "launch special" on Upwork:
   > "I'm building my Upwork profile this month and offering my first 3 clients a 20% discount on any package. Happy to chat about your needs."
2. On LinkedIn, post about it:
   > "Launching my IT consulting practice this month. Offering free 30-minute IT audits to my first 5 connections who are interested. DM me."
3. The goal is to get ANY paid work, even at a discount — the review/testimonial is worth more than the revenue right now

**Done when:** Discount offer posted on at least 2 channels

**Time:** 30 minutes

---

### Task 25: Close and Deliver

**Steps:**
1. When a client says yes:
   - Send contract (Task 5 template)
   - Send invoice for 50% deposit (Wave)
   - Wait for deposit before starting
   - Schedule kickoff call
2. Deliver the work following the Project Execution Playbook in the Operations Runbook
3. After delivery:
   - Send final invoice
   - Request a testimonial/review
   - Set a 30-day follow-up reminder

**Done when:** First engagement completed and paid

**Time:** Varies by project (typically 3–8 hours for a starter package)

---

- [ ] Metrics reviewed, strategy adjusted
- [ ] Discount/launch offer posted
- [ ] First client closed (or strong pipeline for Week 7)
- [ ] First engagement delivered (if timeline allows)

**Total time: ~4–8 hours (depending on client work)**

## Post-Launch: Weekly Maintenance Rhythm

Once launched, follow this weekly cadence (from the Operations Runbook):

| Day | Task | Time |
|-----|------|------|
| Monday | Check retainer clients, respond to inquiries, review pipeline | 30 min |
| Tue–Thu | Client delivery work | As scheduled |
| Friday | Log hours, send invoices, 1 LinkedIn post | 30 min |
| Saturday | Bid on 3–5 Upwork gigs, respond to proposals | 30 min |

**Monthly:**
- Send retainer reports
- Review income vs. target
- Adjust pricing or services if needed
- File quarterly estimated taxes (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15)
