### Which Program Fits Your Goals?

**BS Computer Science** — Best if you want to become a software engineer or full-stack developer. Covers algorithms, data structures, C++, full-stack development (CS-465/470). Most rigorous and most respected for engineering roles. Downside: least Sophia-friendly, highest cost, no cloud-specific courses.

**BS IT (Software Dev concentration)** — Good middle ground. Covers Python, Java, C#/.NET, databases, networking, and project management. More applied and practical than CS. Decent Sophia savings.

**BA Information Technology** — Fastest and cheapest path to graduation. Has cloud computing (IT-423), security (IT-253), and networking built in. 33 free elective credits absorb maximum Sophia transfers. Best if you want to graduate fast and let certs + projects speak louder than the degree name.

**BS Data Analytics** — Only if you want to pivot toward data/BI. Not aligned with your cloud/engineering goals.

### Recommendation

Given your goals (cloud, Python, React, TypeScript, transition to engineering):

| Priority | Recommendation | Why |
|----------|---------------|-----|
| **1st choice** | **BS Computer Science** | Most technically rigorous, covers full-stack dev and algorithms. Engineering roles value this most. Your certs + projects cover the cloud gap. |
| **2nd choice** | **BA Information Technology** | Cheapest and fastest. Has cloud computing and security courses. Get the degree done, then let your portfolio and certs differentiate you. |
| **3rd choice** | **BS IT (Software Dev)** | Good middle ground but overlaps heavily with skills you can learn on your own (C#/.NET, networking). |

## Part 5: SNHU Format — What to Expect

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Term length** | 8 weeks |
| **Terms per year** | 6 |
| **Format** | 100% online, asynchronous (no live classes) |
| **Weekly rhythm** | Discussion posts due Thursday, assignments due Sunday |
| **Workload** | ~15-16 hrs/week per course |
| **Interaction** | Discussion boards, instructor feedback, no live lectures |
| **Tools** | Brightspace LMS, Zybooks (for CS), virtual labs |
| **Capstone (CS-499)** | Build an ePortfolio on GitHub Pages with 3 enhanced artifacts + code review video |

### Tips from SNHU Students

- Start with 1 course your first term to learn the format
- Front-load your week — don't save everything for Sunday
- The CS courses get significantly harder after CS-300 (Data Structures)
- CS-499 capstone requires you to revisit previous projects, so save your work
- Discussion posts feel tedious but are typically 10-15% of your grade
