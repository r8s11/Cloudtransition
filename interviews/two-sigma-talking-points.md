# Two Sigma — Manager Conversation Talking Points

> Prep doc for discussing internal growth and flexibility. Read through, personalize, and practice before the meeting.

---

## Before the Meeting

**Request the meeting casually:** _"Hey [Manager], do you have 20 minutes this week? I'd like to talk about my growth path here and get your advice on a few things."_

Don't frame it as a negotiation. Frame it as a career conversation — managers expect these.

---

## Part 1: Express Commitment + Ask About Growth

**Goal:** Show you're invested in Two Sigma, not looking for the exit. Then open the door to internal mobility.

**Your real situation (for your eyes only):**
You relocated to NYC and Two Sigma was the opportunity that got you in the door. You're grateful for that. But the scope of this role is narrower than what you've done before — you've built systems from scratch at previous companies, and here the work is more routine. Your colleagues have been in the same seat for years, which tells you this role may not have a natural growth path. You need to find out if that's really the case, or if there's a path you're not seeing.

**Open with appreciation, then pivot to curiosity:**

> "I appreciate the opportunity here — working at this scale has been a great learning experience. When I moved to New York, I wanted to get into a strong environment, and Two Sigma delivered on that. Now that I'm settling in, I'm thinking about the longer term. What does the growth path look like for someone in my role? Are there teams or positions where my background could be a fit?"

**Then show what you bring beyond support:**

> "I want to make sure you know what I've done beyond desktop support. At a biotech startup I built Jira Service Management from scratch — designed all the workflows and SLAs. I deployed GitHub Enterprise for their dev team. I stood up SQL Server infrastructure for their researchers. At Mass General I co-developed a bug-tracking web app. I also hold AWS and Azure certifications. I'm wondering if there's a way to put more of that experience to use here — maybe with the Technology Operations Engineering team or in platform/cloud operations."

**Key points to weave in naturally:**
- You built Jira Service Management from zero at Ventus (not just used it — *designed the workflows*)
- You deployed GitHub Enterprise for a dev team
- You stood up SQL Server infrastructure for researchers
- You co-built a web app at Mass General Hospital
- You hold AWS CCP and AZ-900, and you're studying for AZ-204
- You're bilingual (English/Spanish) — useful for global teams

**What you're really listening for:**
- Does your manager seem surprised by your background? (Means they don't know what you can do — that's fixable)
- Do they name specific teams or people? (That's a real path)
- Do they give vague encouragement with no specifics? (That's a polite "no")
- Do they confirm that colleagues have been in the same role for years? (That's your answer)

---

## Part 2: Ask About Flexibility and Compensation

**Goal:** Explore hybrid/remote options and better pay without sounding disengaged or desperate.

**Your real situation (for your eyes only):**
You have debt to clear before school starts. You're considering barbacking on nights/weekends to make that happen. If Two Sigma can offer more pay or flexibility, that could reduce or eliminate the need for a second job. But you can't say any of that directly.

**Only bring this up after the growth conversation goes well.** If the mood is right:

> "I also wanted to ask about two things — flexibility and compensation. Are there roles or arrangements that allow for hybrid work? And is there room to revisit compensation as I take on more or grow into a different role? I'm investing in my career right now — starting a degree program and building toward more cloud certifications — and I want to make sure I'm set up to give my best here."

**If they ask why flexibility matters:**

> "I'm planning to start a degree program and I'm managing some financial goals. Having a little flexibility in my schedule would help me balance everything while staying fully committed here."

**If they ask about compensation specifically:**

> "I want to make sure my compensation reflects the experience I bring. I've been doing this work for 7 years across multiple industries, and I have certifications that go beyond what the current role requires. If there's a path to a role with more responsibility and better compensation, I'd love to know what that looks like."

**What NOT to say:**
- Don't mention the barback job — it undermines your professional positioning
- Don't say "I need more money" — let the growth conversation create that opening naturally
- Don't frame it as an ultimatum or threat to leave
- Don't compare yourself to colleagues or mention that people seem stuck

---

## Part 3: If They Ask What You're Looking For

Be ready with a clear answer:

> "Ideally, I'd love to grow into an infrastructure or cloud operations role here — something where I can use my cloud certifications and my experience building systems. I'm actively working toward that — studying for AZ-204, writing Python scripts, learning Docker and CI/CD. If there's a path to that kind of role at Two Sigma, I'd rather grow here than look elsewhere."

**If they ask "Are you thinking about leaving?":**

> "I'm not looking to leave — I'm looking to grow. I moved to New York and chose Two Sigma intentionally. But I also want to be honest that I have more to offer than what the current role asks of me. I'd rather have that conversation with you than just quietly look around."

This is direct without being threatening. It shows loyalty, honesty, and self-awareness — all things a good manager respects.

---

## Possible Outcomes & How to Respond

| Outcome | Your Response |
|---------|--------------|
| **"There might be openings"** | Ask: "What would I need to do to be considered? Who should I talk to?" |
| **"Not right now"** | Ask: "What skills or experience would make me a strong candidate when something opens up?" |
| **"Your role doesn't lead there"** | Ask: "Are there lateral moves or internal transfers I should know about?" |
| **"We can look at hybrid"** | Express gratitude, ask about the process to formalize it |
| **"No flexibility available"** | Thank them, don't push. You now know where you stand. |

---

## After the Meeting

- Send a short follow-up email thanking them for their time
- If they mentioned specific teams or people, reach out within a week
- Document what was discussed in your reflections folder
- **Decision point:** Based on what you learn, you'll know whether to invest more here or accelerate your external search

## What This Conversation Really Tells You

This meeting is as much for **your** information as theirs:

| What you hear | What it means | Your next move |
|---|---|---|
| Specific team names, intros offered | Real path exists | Pursue it aggressively |
| "Let me look into it" + follow-up within a week | Genuine interest | Give it 1-2 weeks, then check in |
| Vague encouragement, no specifics | Polite deflection | Start prioritizing external options |
| "That's not really how things work here" | Confirmed ceiling | You have your answer — plan accordingly |
| Colleagues in the same role for years comes up | The role is the role | Don't wait for something that won't come |

**No outcome is bad.** Every answer gives you information to make a better decision.

---

## Mindset

You've been at Two Sigma for ~2 months. That's early, so:

- **You're not entitled to anything yet** — but you ARE entitled to ask questions about growth
- **Curiosity is always welcome** — framing this as "I want to grow here" is never a bad look
- **The worst outcome is useful information** — even a "no" tells you where you stand so you can plan accordingly
- **You're not stuck** — you have options. You're exploring this call, you're planning school, you have 7 years of experience. This conversation is one input, not your only path.

You're not asking for a favor. You're having a professional conversation about your career. That's what strong employees do.
