# Initial Call Prep — IT Support / Desktop Support Associate

> NYC Onsite | $70K–$90K | M-F

---

## What They'll Ask (and How to Answer)

### 1. "Tell me about yourself" (1-2 minutes max)

> "I'm an IT Support Engineer with about 7 years of experience across finance, healthcare, and biotech. Right now I'm at Two Sigma in New York doing desktop support for 1,700 end users — hardware deployments, troubleshooting, Jira ticket management, and white-glove support up to C-suite. Before that I worked remote for a scientific instruments company supporting 200 users globally, and before that I was at Foundation Medicine, Mass General Hospital, and a few biotech startups in Boston. I'm very comfortable with the M365 ecosystem, Active Directory, Intune, and all the standard desktop support tooling. I'm also AWS and Azure certified."

**Keep it tight.** Don't list every job — hit the highlights and land on what's relevant to them.

---

### 2. "Why are you looking to make a move?"

This is the most important question. You need a clean, honest answer that doesn't raise red flags.

**Your story (the real one, framed well):**

You relocated to NYC, took the first strong opportunity at Two Sigma to get established, and now that you're settled you want to be intentional about your next step. You've done more than support in previous roles — you've built systems from scratch — and you want a role that uses your full experience.

**Primary answer:**

> "When I relocated to New York, I prioritized getting into a strong environment quickly. Two Sigma gave me that — I'm supporting 1,700 users at a top finance firm, and I've learned a lot about operating at that scale. But now that I'm settled, I want to be more intentional about my next step. I'm looking for a role where my full experience is being used and where there's a clear path to grow."

**If they probe deeper — "What's missing in your current role?":**

> "The work itself is solid, but the scope is narrower than what I've done before. In previous roles I built things from the ground up — Jira Service Management, GitHub Enterprise, SQL Server infrastructure. I'm looking for an environment where I can take on more responsibility over time — not just support, but improving systems and processes."

**If they ask "What kind of growth are you looking for?":**

> "I'm at a point where I want more ownership. I've built systems from scratch before and I want to do that kind of work again. I'm also actively building my cloud and automation skills — I'm AWS and Azure certified and working toward AZ-204. I want to be somewhere that rewards that kind of initiative."

**The formula to remember:** "I moved for practical reasons → I made the most of it → now I'm ready to be strategic."

**What NOT to say:**
- ~~"It was my only option"~~ — sounds desperate even if true
- ~~"I'm overqualified"~~ — sounds arrogant to someone who doesn't know you yet
- ~~"There's no growth"~~ or ~~"people have been stuck there for years"~~ — sounds like blaming the employer
- Don't say anything negative about Two Sigma
- Don't mention needing money or a second job
- Don't mention the role is a step down in scope — frame it as a deliberate, positive choice
- Don't say you're "just exploring" — that signals low intent

---

### 3. "Walk me through your experience with [specific technology]"

You'll likely get asked about 2-3 of these. Keep answers to 30-60 seconds each:

**Windows 11:**
> "I support Windows 10 and 11 daily at Two Sigma. Automated PC builds, imaging, troubleshooting driver issues, OS upgrades, Group Policy — it's bread and butter for me."

**Office 365 / M365:**
> "I've managed M365 environments ranging from 100 to 1,700 users. At Ventus Therapeutics I co-managed the entire tenant — licensing, provisioning, security configs. At Two Sigma I work within a larger M365 ecosystem and handle user-facing issues daily."

**Active Directory:**
> "I've worked with AD across every role — account creation, group management, password resets, locked accounts, Group Policy troubleshooting. At PSM Partners I administered AD as part of managing the client's full infrastructure."

**Intune:**
> "I've used Intune for device management and compliance policies. At PSM Partners it was part of the core stack alongside Defender and Azure AD."

**Teams / Zoom / Conference Rooms:**
> "I set up and maintain conference room AV at Two Sigma and did the same at Pillar Biosciences for 50+ person events. I'm comfortable with Teams, Zoom, and the hardware side — cameras, displays, audio systems."

**Exchange:**
> "Distribution lists, shared mailboxes, mail flow troubleshooting, and Outlook profile issues. Dealt with this at every role."

---

### 4. "What's your salary expectation?"

**Your target: $90K (top of range)**

You have 7+ years, cloud certs, and you're currently supporting 1,700 users at a top-tier finance firm. You should be at the top of this range.

**How to say it:**

> "Based on my experience level and the market, I'm looking in the $85-90K range. I'm open to discussing the full compensation picture though — benefits, growth path, and the work itself all matter to me."

**If they push back or ask if you'd accept lower:**

> "I'm flexible, but with 7 years of experience and current cloud certifications, I want to make sure the compensation reflects that. What does the full range look like for this role?"

**Do NOT:**
- Say your current salary first if you can avoid it
- Say "I'm open to anything" — that signals desperation
- Go below $80K — you'd be undercutting your experience

---

### 5. "Are you comfortable being onsite 5 days a week?"

> "Yes, absolutely. I'm onsite five days a week now at Two Sigma. I'm based in Brooklyn so commuting into Manhattan is already part of my routine."

---

### 6. "Do you have any questions for me?"

Always ask 2-3 questions. This shows engagement:

1. **"Can you tell me about the team I'd be working with? How big is the support team and how is the work distributed?"**
2. **"What does a typical day look like in this role?"**
3. **"What does growth look like in this position? Are there opportunities to take on more responsibility over time?"**

The third question is the most important — it tells them you're thinking long-term and signals ambition without arrogance.

- **Match their energy** — if they're casual, be casual. If formal, be formal.
- **Don't oversell** — you have 7 years, let the experience speak

  > "Hi [Name], thanks for taking the time to chat today. I enjoyed learning more about the role and the team. Based on our conversation, I think my experience supporting enterprise environments — especially in finance and healthcare — would be a strong fit. Looking forward to next steps."
- Log the call in your interviews folder
- Note anything they mentioned about timeline, team size, or next rounds
