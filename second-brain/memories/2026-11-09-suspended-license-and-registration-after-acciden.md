what can be done?
There are three separate problems here, and they can be fixed one at a time. I'm not a lawyer, so treat this as a map rather than legal advice.

**1. The registration suspension is probably the child support, not the insurance.**
Massachusetts law lets the RMV suspend more than your license over child support. If you don't pay child support owed to the Department of Revenue's Child Support Services Division, the RMV can suspend or revoke your license and/or your registration indefinitely. So the registration likely got pulled in with the license. To fix both:
- Call DOR Child Support at **(800) 332-2733** and ask what will lift the suspension: paying in full, or a payment arrangement. DOR's own letters say reinstatement is triggered when the person pays the debt in full or makes consistent child support payments.
- If you think the arrears figure is wrong, ask about a hearing. DOR's hearing is the only administrative way to contest the delinquency or the suspension.
- Once DOR clears you, check with the RMV ((857) 368-8000 or (800) 858-3926) for any reinstatement fees.
- Ask the RMV to confirm the exact reason for the registration suspension. If it turns out to be an insurance flag after all, GEICO can send proof of coverage to clear it.

**2. The car claim: your mom not being on the policy probably doesn't matter.**
Your mom wasn't driving, her friend was. What matters is whether that driver had permission. Permissive use generally covers someone not listed on your policy who occasionally drives your car, and collision coverage can apply if your policy includes it and doesn't exclude permissive use. Steps:
- Open a claim with GEICO now if you haven't, and be completely truthful about who was driving and how the car came to be in use.
- Check whether you have **collision** coverage. Without it, GEICO won't pay for your totaled car unless the other driver was at fault, in which case you claim against *their* insurer.
- Expect to pay your deductible, and the claim may raise your rate at renewal.

**3. Risks to watch for.**
- **Timing of the registration suspension.** If the registration was already suspended on the day of the accident, GEICO or the other side's insurer may raise it. That's the point where a short consultation with a lawyer is worth it.
- **The driver's license status.** Confirm your mom's friend had a valid license.
- **Garaging address.** Make sure what GEICO has on file matches where the car is actually kept. A mismatch can give an insurer grounds to dispute a claim.
- **Don't drive yourself** until the license is reinstated. Driving on a suspended license in Massachusetts can bring steep fines and possibly jail.
- **Plates and policy.** Once the total-loss payout is settled, cancel the registration and turn in the plates before you cancel the insurance. Massachusetts usually wants the plate return receipt to stop coverage cleanly.

Was the accident the friend's fault, or the other driver's? That decides whose insurance pays for your car, and I can help you work out the next step from there.
