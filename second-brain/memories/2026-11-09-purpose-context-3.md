**Purpose & context**

Roosevelt works in desktop support at a hedge fund as a contractor and is actively pursuing a career transition into security engineering and cloud infrastructure. A key differentiator in his strategy is leveraging finance industry domain knowledge alongside technical credentials — a combination he's been advised is rare and highly marketable. His commute (roughly an hour each way) has been identified as valuable study time.

**Current state**

Roosevelt holds an SSCP (Systems Security Certified Practitioner) from ISC², which serves as the foundation for his certification and career strategy. Based on this, a modified 14-week plan has been scoped out:

- **Weeks 1–8:** Security-first portfolio projects — an enterprise IT asset management system and a SOC-style security monitoring dashboard
- **Weeks 9–11:** AZ-500 (Azure Security Engineer Associate) certification
- **Following:** CISSP exam, targeting Associate of ISC² status

Target credential stack: **SSCP + AZ-500 + CISSP Associate**, positioning Roosevelt for security engineering roles in finance.

He has confirmed interest in both a security engineering focus and a finance domain knowledge component as strategic directions.

**On the horizon**

Open questions that were pending at the end of the last conversation:
- Recency of the SSCP (to assess whether renewal or continuing education is a near-term concern)
- Level of interest in pursuing full CISSP vs. Associate of ISC² status
- Tenure at the current hedge fund (relevant for framing domain experience on applications)
- Preferred starting point within the modified plan

**Key learnings & principles**

- Roosevelt's SSCP is meaningfully more advanced than entry-level security certifications — plans and project complexity should reflect that baseline, not underestimate it
- The combination of operational security knowledge, hedge fund exposure, and cloud security credentials is a strong differentiator for finance-sector security engineering roles
- Finance domain knowledge should be treated as a strategic asset, not just background context

**Approach & patterns**

- Roosevelt is pursuing a dual-track strategy: technical credentialing (certifications + portfolio projects) paired with domain specialization (finance industry knowledge)
- Commute time is being treated as structured study/learning time
- Planning tends to be structured and milestone-oriented, with clear sequencing across weeks
