## Key learnings and principles

- [stated] The 8-rep pull-up baseline warranted skipping Phase 1 entirely — programming should match demonstrated capacity, not assumed starting points
- [stated] Volume management is a key constraint: high pull-up workload means handstand and accessory programming must be calibrated carefully to avoid overreaching
- [stated] Supplements are secondary to foundational recovery habits (protein intake, sleep quality); introducing one at a time allows Roosevelt to isolate effects
- [stated] Chest-to-wall progressions are prioritized over back-to-wall for handstand development

## Approach and patterns

- [stated] Roosevelt responds well to structured, phased programming with clear prerequisites, progression gates, and explicit pass/fail criteria
- [stated] Prefers comprehensive session-level detail: tempo notation, coaching cues, and form criteria (e.g., verified by side-on video)
- [stated] Check-in prompts and logging structures (soreness, energy, rep tracking) are built into programming to support self-monitoring
- [stated] Deliverables are often exported as formatted documents (Markdown or Word) for reference outside of conversation
- [stated] A structured coaching framework (Scope → Evidence → Attack → Verify → Report) has been applied to program design, separating analysis from the final deliverable

## Tools and resources

- [stated] Training documents exported as Markdown files and Word documents
- [stated] Tempo notation system (A-B-C-D format with time under tension per set) used for resistance training prescription
