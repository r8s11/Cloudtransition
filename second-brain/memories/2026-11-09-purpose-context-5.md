**Purpose & context**

Roosevelt is pursuing three integrated fitness goals: improving sprint speed, achieving 25 consecutive pull-ups, and progressing toward a freestanding handstand (with headstand as a stepping stone). Training is structured around a consistent weekly schedule with specific days designated for each modality, and programming decisions account for cumulative fatigue across goals.

**Current state**

- **Weekly training structure:** Sprint sessions Monday/Thursday; gym workouts (alternating Workout A/B) Tuesday/Friday; Wednesday/weekends for rest or active recovery; daily 5-minute headstand practice; daily grease-the-groove pull-ups (4–5 reps every 2–3 hours)
- **Handstand prep block:** A 4-week structured program is in place, integrating two dedicated handstand sessions (Wednesday/Saturday), short scapular primers before Tuesday/Friday gym sessions, and a Sunday mobility/wrist restoration session. Key programming choices include prioritizing chest-to-wall over back-to-wall progressions and gating advanced core work (ab wheel, full hollow hold) behind prerequisite benchmarks
- **Pull-ups:** Roosevelt tested at 8 reps baseline, placing directly into Phase 2 (Volume Build); programming involves 5 sets of near-max reps on gym days plus daily grease-the-groove work
- **Recovery:** A structured supplement plan has been established, prioritizing creatine monohydrate, protein, and magnesium, introduced sequentially to monitor response

**On the horizon**

- Week 4 of the handstand prep block includes a formal test day with explicit pass/fail exit standards, which will determine progression
- Ongoing tracking of pull-up rep counts and recovery quality (soreness, energy) across sessions
- Potential advancement from headstand toward freestanding handstand as benchmarks are met

**Key learnings & principles**

- Pull-up baseline of 8 reps warranted skipping Phase 1 entirely — programming should match demonstrated capacity, not assumed starting points
- Volume management is a key constraint: high pull-up workload means handstand and accessory programming must be calibrated carefully to avoid overreaching
- Supplements are secondary to foundational recovery habits (protein intake, sleep quality); introducing one at a time allows Roosevelt to isolate effects
- Chest-to-wall progressions are prioritized over back-to-wall for handstand development

**Approach & patterns**

- Roosevelt responds well to structured, phased programming with clear prerequisites, progression gates, and explicit pass/fail criteria
- Prefers comprehensive session-level detail: tempo notation, coaching cues, and form criteria (e.g., verified by side-on video)
- Check-in prompts and logging structures (soreness, energy, rep tracking) are built into programming to support self-monitoring
- Deliverables are often exported as formatted documents (Markdown or Word) for reference outside of conversation
- A structured coaching framework (Scope → Evidence → Attack → Verify → Report) has been applied to program design, separating analysis from the final deliverable

**Tools & resources**

- Training documents exported as Markdown files and Word documents
- Tempo notation system (A-B-C-D format with time under tension per set) used for resistance training prescription
