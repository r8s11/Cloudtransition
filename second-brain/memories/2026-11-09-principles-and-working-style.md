## Key learnings & principles

- [stated] Concrete ask over vague invitation: specific, actionable invites consistently outperform open-ended or standing offers — in dating contexts and as a general principle Roosevelt already operates with
- [stated] Science before tactics before critique: the recommended FORGE reading sequence prioritizes foundational research, then applied strategy, then critical deconstruction
- [stated] Ethics as architecture, not afterthought: guardrails against manipulation, entitlement, and objectification are hard-coded into ROOK and woven through curriculum design, not treated as optional commentary
- [stated] Counter-read protocol: provocative or controversial texts (e.g. The Rational Male) are never consumed in isolation and are always paired with critical counterweights (e.g. bell hooks, Laura Bates)
- [stated] Duplicate risk is real: multiple sessions have rebuilt overlapping FORGE artifacts, so checking for existing files before generating new ones is an established protocol
- [stated] Covert "testing" mindsets undermine authenticity: in dating contexts, evaluative or strategic framing can erode the genuine quality of interactions

## Approach & working patterns

- [stated] Roosevelt's aesthetic preferences — minimalist, data-dense, structured, gamified — shape all FORGE artifacts
- [stated] Prefers synthesized, multi-source answers over single-reference responses
- [stated] Works in a trigger-word or shorthand style (e.g. "Forge," "Start") to initiate full build sessions, relying on Claude to reconstruct context and execute
- [stated] Values practical, applied outputs: actionable frameworks, deployable prompts, trackable trackers
- [stated] Engages Claude for both real-time situational advice (e.g. live social scenarios) and large-scale system architecture
- [stated] Copy and communication preferences lean casual, inbound-generating, and question-forward rather than polished or declarative
