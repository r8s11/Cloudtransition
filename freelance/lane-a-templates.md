## Payment terms — pick by channel, they are not interchangeable

Getting this wrong on Upwork is a **terms-of-service violation**, not a style choice.

### Upwork (or any platform-sourced client)

- **Fixed-price contract with milestones. The first milestone must be funded in escrow before
  any work starts.** Escrow funding is the platform's version of a deposit.
- **All payment stays on-platform.** Never invoice an Upwork-sourced client directly, never ask
  for a deposit outside the platform, never move the relationship off Upwork to dodge the fee.
  It violates their ToS, voids payment protection, and risks the account you're building
  reviews on.
- Phrase it as: *"I'll set up the first milestone; work begins once it's funded."* Never
  *"40% up front."*
- **Escrow-funded is NOT cash received.** Funds release on client approval, then sit through a
  security period before withdrawal. Plan on roughly 1–2 weeks from approval to usable cash.
- Accept the ~10% fee as the cost of dispute protection and review-building. It is priced into
  the tables in [lane-a-services.md](lane-a-services.md).

### Direct clients (warm network, MSP subcontract, local)

- **40% deposit on signature, remainder on delivery** (or per milestone on P2).
- Work starts when the deposit **clears** — not when it's promised or "sent".
- No platform fee, and payment can clear same-week, which is why direct is the channel to
  favour when a deadline matters.
- Get scope in writing anyway. You have no platform dispute process here; the written scope
  *is* your protection.

### Both

Count money on the **date it lands in your account**. Escrow funded, milestone approved, and
invoice sent are all $0 in the ledger in [profiles-2026-08.md](profiles-2026-08.md).

## P1 proposal — "Day-1 IT Takeover Audit"

Two channels, **two different payment mechanics** — see the Payment terms box below before
sending. Keep it under one screen; length reads as padding.

> **Subject / opening line:** Day-1 IT Audit — know exactly what you inherited, in one week
>
> `<Name>` — you mentioned `<specific detail from their posting>`. That's the exact situation
> this package is built for.
>
> _Examples of that detail: "no documentation from the last IT person" · "just acquired a
> 40-person team" · "first IT hire starts next month"._
>
> **What you get (fixed scope):**
>
> 1. **Discovery audit** — inventory of systems, accounts, licenses, vendors, and access across
>    your environment, with gaps flagged where nothing exists to inventory.
> 2. **Stakeholder map** — who approves spend, who owns which system, who to escalate to.
>    People are referenced anonymously (S-01, S-02); the name mapping stays with you.
> 3. **Prioritized risk register** — every finding scored by likelihood and impact, so you're
>    working the top five instead of all forty.
> 4. **30-60-90 plan** — sequenced actions with owners, derived from the risk register.
>
> **Timeline:** <5-7> business days from access.
> **Price:** $`<1,500-2,500>` fixed. Payment terms per the box below — `<escrow milestone / deposit>`.
> **Not included:** implementing the fixes, tool purchases, ongoing support. Implementation is
> quotable separately once we both know what's actually there.
> **I need from you:** read access to `<IdP/MDM/ticketing>`, ~2 hours total of SME time, and a
> list of tools you know you pay for.
>
> **Why me:** 10+ years running IT in finance, healthcare, and biotech — currently managing
> enterprise Windows refresh and Intune/Autopilot deployments day to day. I work from a
> standardized operational playbook, which is why this takes a week rather than a month.
>
> Happy to do a 15-minute call first if you'd rather scope it live.
>
> — Roosevelt Segura

**Notes on using this:**

- Line 1 must reference something specific from their posting or it reads as a mass proposal.
- Lead with the deliverable list, not your bio. Buyers scan for "what do I get".
- The exclusions paragraph prevents the most common scope creep and signals experience.
- Never name a current or former employer. "finance, healthcare, and biotech" is the ceiling.
- **Never start unfunded.** On Upwork that means the first milestone is funded in escrow; direct,
  it means the deposit has cleared. A client who won't do either is the one who disputes later.

## P2 proposal — "IT Operations Documentation Buildout"

Pitch this **after** a P1, or to a client who already knows their problem. Cold P2 rarely lands.

> **Opening:** Your `<onboarding/offboarding/asset tracking>` runs on one person's memory. Here's
> the fix, documented and yours to keep.
>
> **What you get (fixed scope):**
>
> 1. **SOP engine** — a standard template plus the governance model: who owns each SOP, review
>    cadence, access tiers, how they get updated instead of going stale.
> 2. **<6-10> SOPs**, chosen with you. Common set: hardware intake and tagging, deployment,
>    retire/sanitize/dispose, employee onboarding, offboarding, access recertification.
> 3. **Asset register schema** — the canonical field list, so your inventory tool (or
>    spreadsheet) tracks what actually matters.
> 4. **Tool mapping** — how the above maps onto your specific stack (<Intune / Jamf / Snipe-IT /
>    Jira Service Management>).
>
> **Timeline:** <2-3> weeks, three milestones: engine → SOP set → schema and mapping.
> **Price:** $<3,500-5,000> fixed, billed per milestone.
> **Not included:** tool implementation or migration, licensing, ongoing maintenance
> (available as a monthly retainer once this lands).
> **I need from you:** a walkthrough of current process per area (~1 hour each), and a named
> approver who can sign off each milestone.
>
> Everything is delivered in your naming and your tooling — it's yours, editable, no lock-in.

**Notes:**

- Three milestones, three payments. Never one payment on delivery.
- "No lock-in" preempts the objection that a consultant's docs die when they leave.
- If this must carry the $3,000 sprint alone, it needs to be **$4,000+ and sold direct** — see
  the math in [lane-a-services.md](lane-a-services.md#p2).

## Warm-network messages

Send **five**. This is the highest-conversion channel you have and
[networking/contacts.yml](../networking/contacts.yml) is currently empty — that's the gap to close.

Rules: no blast. One message per person, each with a specific detail. Do not attach anything.
Ask for a _referral_, not a job — it's a lower-cost yes and it doesn't read as distress.

### 1 — Former colleague (knows your work)

> Hey `<Name>` — hope things are good at `<company>`. Quick one: I've started taking on freelance IT
> operations work on the side — the documentation and process side specifically (SOPs, asset
> lifecycle, onboarding/offboarding, Day-1 audits when someone inherits a mess).
>
> If you run into anyone whose IT runs on tribal knowledge, or a company that just lost their
> only IT person, I'd appreciate the intro. Happy to return the favor anytime.

### 2 — Friend outside IT (referral reach, not technical judgment)

> Hey `<Name>` — I'm picking up freelance work on the side, helping small companies get their IT
> organized: writing down how things actually work so it doesn't all live in one person's head.
>
> Usually the sign is a company where "ask `<person>`" is the process. If you hear anyone
> complaining about that at work, send them my way?

### 3 — Former manager / senior contact

> Hi `<Name>` — I'm doing some freelance IT operations consulting alongside my current contract,
> focused on operational documentation: SOP frameworks, asset lifecycle, joiner/mover/leaver
> process, and Day-1 audits for teams inheriting an undocumented environment.
>
> I've built a fairly complete vendor-neutral playbook for this over the past year, so I can
> turn it around quickly. If it'd be useful to anyone in your network, I'd welcome the intro —
> and I'd value your read on how you'd position it.

_(#3 doubles as a positioning check from someone senior. Ask for the opinion, not just the intro.)_

### 4 — MSP / IT services contact

> Hi `<Name>` — do you ever subcontract documentation work? I'm freelancing on the side and
> documentation is specifically what I do: SOP libraries, asset schemas, onboarding/offboarding
> runbooks, tool mappings for Intune/Jamf/Snipe-IT/Jira SM.
>
> It's the work most MSPs don't want to staff for. Fixed-scope, white-label, delivered in your
> client's naming. Worth a conversation?

### 5 — Peer in a small company (direct prospect, soft ask)

> Hey `<Name>` — random question: how does `<their company>` handle IT onboarding and offboarding?
> Documented, or is it "whoever knows"?
>
> Asking because I've started doing freelance work fixing exactly that, and small companies are
> usually surprised how much is undocumented until someone leaves. If it's a sore spot, happy to
> talk through it — no pitch.

**After sending:** log every one in [networking/contacts.yml](../networking/contacts.yml) with
the date. Follow up **once** after 5–7 days, then stop.

## Discovery call — 15 minutes

Five questions. Their answers scope the proposal and tell you which package to pitch.

1. What triggered this — someone leaving, an audit, growth, an incident?
2. What's documented today, and where does it live?
3. How many people, how many devices, and which tools? (`<IdP>`, `<MDM>`, ticketing)
4. What breaks most often?
5. Who signs off, and what's the budget range?

**Routing:** no documentation and something just broke → **P1**. Knows the problem, has budget,
wants it fixed → **P2**. Already documented, needs upkeep → **P3**.

Close with: _"I'll send a fixed-scope proposal by `<day>`."_ Then send it within 24 hours — speed
is a differentiator on Upwork, where most freelancers take days.

## Per-engagement confidentiality checklist

Run this on **day 1 of every engagement**, before any work:

- [ ] Add the client's name, domain, and any employee names to `~/.config/playbook-exclusions.txt`
- [ ] Create a **separate private repo** for their deliverables — never the playbook repo
- [ ] Copy `tools/validate.py` + `tools/hooks/pre-commit` into it; `git config core.hooksPath tools/hooks`
- [ ] Confirm `PLAYBOOK_EXCLUSION_LIST` is exported in the shell you'll actually work in
- [ ] Deliverables carry _their_ tokens and naming — never a playbook file verbatim
- [ ] On close: anything worth keeping goes back to the playbook **rewritten generic**, never
      paste-then-scrub (the `pstools` lesson — a scrubbed paste still lives in git history)
